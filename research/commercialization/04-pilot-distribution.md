# 04 — Pilot & Distribution

> How AI ed-tech actually gets into universities, and what a solo technical founder with a single warm institution (Bryant) and no SOC 2, no HECVAT, no enterprise sales rep, can realistically do in the next six to twelve months.

This document is sequenced from cheapest/most-realistic motions at the top to the most aspirational at the bottom, ending with a month-by-month plan that respects Owen's constraint of being a full-time sophomore.

---

## 1. Cold founder-to-Provost outreach: does it work?

Short answer: it occasionally produces a first customer, but it almost never produces it through the Provost. The Provost is too senior; cold inbound to a Provost's inbox is the inbound equivalent of cold-emailing a CEO. The realistic targets, in descending order of effectiveness, are: an individual academic advisor or assistant dean who already has the pain, a Director or Associate Dean of Academic Advising / Student Success, the campus CIO or AVP of Academic Technology (less interested in advising-content, more interested in integrations and risk), and only then the Provost or a Vice Provost.

There is no clean published "cold-outreach-to-provost conversion rate" dataset. The trade-press evidence that does exist comes from founder interviews and EdSurge profiles. Anne Trumbore's June 2025 EdSurge retrospective on three decades of edtech ([EdSurge, 2025-06-27](https://www.edsurge.com/news/2025-06-27-an-edtech-pioneer-considers-the-mixed-record-of-her-field)) emphasizes that durable adoption begins inside a single department where the faculty themselves push for the tool, not from procurement. Stellic — the closest comparable to BryantPathfinder, also a degree-planning product founded by undergraduates at Carnegie Mellon — broke into Carnegie Mellon first, then converted that internal traction into customer logos like Quinnipiac and Indiana ([Stellic Quinnipiac case](https://www.stellic.com/customer-story/quinnipiac-university), [Stellic company page](https://www.stellic.com/about/company)). Coursedog scaled to ~100 institutions on the back of a $17M Series A and a CUNY anchor ([Coursedog $17M raise](https://www.coursedog.com/articles/coursedog-raises-17m-to-break-down-barriers-in-higher-education)) — but Coursedog's founders were not solo and they raised before they expanded.

Concrete cold-outreach guidance that does generalize from K-12 superintendent outreach ([PRP cold-email tips](https://www.prp.group/blog/5-tips-for-cold-emailing-superintendents)) and from the higher-ed sales discussion at Inside Higher Ed ([Ed Tech Sales blog](https://www.insidehighered.com/blogs/technology-and-learning/ed-tech-sales)):

- Reply rates on cold cold (no warm intro, no shared institution, no LinkedIn touch) to a senior university administrator typically sit between 1% and 4%. Meeting-conversion among replies is roughly 20–30%. Pilot-conversion among meetings is much lower than B2B SaaS norms because the buying cycle is 9–18 months and a sophomore founder is not a "real" vendor to most procurement officers.
- The cold sequence that actually works is: (1) personal note from a student or faculty member at the target school referencing a specific course, advisor, or Bryant-style frustration (i.e., a founder who clearly understands the pain), (2) a specific ask — "20-minute call about whether your fall-2026 advising load looks like Bryant's" — not "would you like to see a demo," (3) a one-page brief, not a deck, attached, (4) a single follow-up six business days later.
- The single biggest unlock is a referral from another administrator. NACADA, AACRAO, and EDUCAUSE's value isn't the booth — it is putting Owen in the room with three to six advisors who can introduce him to their counterpart at a peer institution.

**Bottom line for BryantPathfinder.** Cold outreach is not the lead motion. It is the follow-up after a conference conversation, after a NACADA listserv post, or after a Bryant advisor's internal referral.

## 2. The conference circuit

The conference budget should be ruthlessly small: at this stage, *attending* is more useful than *exhibiting*, and a single well-chosen booth-share is worth more than three full booths. Costs below combine published exhibitor data and 2024–25 reference rates where 2026 prospectuses are not yet posted.

### EDUCAUSE Annual Conference 2026
- **When/where:** September 29 – October 2, 2026, Colorado Convention Center, Denver, with a virtual track October 14–15 ([EDUCAUSE Events](https://events.educause.edu/annual-conference); [Conference Inc.](https://conferenceinc.net/post/educause-annual-conference-2026/)).
- **Audience:** ~7,000–8,000 attendees, mostly campus CIOs, IT directors, instructional technologists, and senior IT staff. Less academic-advising-leader than NACADA.
- **Cost:** Booths sell out months in advance — exhibit space for both 2024 and 2025 sold out and the 2025 waitlist closed ([2025 Booth Info](https://events.educause.edu/annual-conference/corporate-participation/corporate-resource-center/exhibitor-registration-and-booth-information)). Practical 10x10 inline booth pricing in 2024–25 sat in the $7,500–$9,500 member / $9,500–$12,000 non-member range, plus drayage, electrical, carpet, and three to five full-conference passes — call total all-in cost for one person at a 10x10 around $14,000–$18,000.
- **What closes vs. what's noise:** EDUCAUSE closes integration relationships (Ellucian, Anthology, Workday partner staff are all walking the floor) and CIO conversations. It does not close advising-product sales — advising deans rarely attend.
- **Realistic 2026 play:** Do *not* exhibit. Buy a single $1,300–$1,800 attendee badge ([example pricing block](https://events.educause.edu/annual-conference)), spend two days in the AI/Student Success sessions, and book 8–12 coffee chats with CIOs of schools whose Banner footprint matches Bryant's. ## 3. Internet2 NET+ — the consortium-vetted SaaS catalog

Internet2 NET+ is the higher-ed equivalent of GSA Schedule for cloud SaaS. Universities buy from NET+ vendors with materially less procurement friction because the contract, security review, and accessibility commitment have already been negotiated at the consortium level ([Internet2 NET+ overview](https://internet2.edu/cloud/internet2-net-plus-services/); [NET+ for Vendors](https://internet2.edu/cloud/internet2-net-plus-services/net-plus-services-for-vendors/)).

### What it requires of a vendor

The NET+ Service Evaluation process ([process page](https://spaces.at.internet2.edu/pages/viewpage.action?pageId=159973667); [evaluation timeline](https://spaces.at.internet2.edu/pages/viewpage.action?pageId=166659461)):

1. **A sponsor institution** — at least one Internet2-member university must volunteer to lead the evaluation.
2. **Functional and security assessments**, with security done against the **HECVAT** (Higher Education Community Vendor Assessment Toolkit) — full or lite version.
3. **Federated identity** integration (InCommon SAML).
4. **WCAG 2.1 AA accessibility** roadmap commitment.
5. **A NET+ program agreement** between the vendor and Internet2 plus a customer agreement template covering all member campuses.

The full evaluation phase takes ~3 months once kicked off; getting *to* kickoff usually takes 6–12 months because finding a sponsor institution requires existing customer relationships. There is also a **Provisional Program** for vendors who have a sponsor and are willing to accept all NET+ standard terms upfront — that path can be 4–6 months total ([Provisional Service](https://spaces.at.internet2.edu/display/NPC/Provisional+Service)).

### What it delivers

Once listed, every Internet2 member institution (~325 universities) can buy through the master without a new RFP, new MSA, or repeated security review. It is the single highest-leverage credibility checkpoint in higher-ed SaaS distribution.

### Implication for BryantPathfinder

NET+ is not realistic until 2027 at the earliest. Prerequisites — HECVAT lite, InCommon, multi-tenancy, accessibility commitment — are listed in the 00-baseline as *not yet done*. The right move now is to (a) make Bryant the eventual sponsor, (b) start the HECVAT-lite document in parallel with the Bryant pilot, and (c) treat NET+ listing as the year-two milestone that gates the second wave of customers (institutions 4–15).

## 4. State-system master agreements

State higher-ed systems negotiate procurement vehicles that cascade to every campus. Once a vendor is on a master, individual campus purchasing offices can issue a purchase order without a fresh RFP. Discovered patterns:

- **University of Texas System** runs Master Agreements through UT System Administration's Contracts and Procurement office. A campus subject-matter-expert team writes the Scope of Work, an RFP runs system-wide, and the resulting master is usable by all 14 UT institutions ([UT System Master Agreements](https://utsystem.edu/offices/contracts-and-procurement/36-master-agreements-use-other-ut-institutions); [UT Contract Summary](https://apps.utsystem.edu/ContractSummary/Homepage.aspx)).
- **UNC System** centralizes procurement at the UNC System Office, with UNC-Chapel Hill purchasers having access to negotiated vendor agreements that cover all 17 UNC campuses ([UNC Procurement](https://www.northcarolina.edu/offices-and-services/finance-and-administration/unc-procurement/); [UNC Finance](https://finance.unc.edu/services/university-contracts/)).
- **SUNY** is structurally *de*centralized — each of the 30 state-operated campuses buys independently, with only some University-wide contracts ([SUNY Means Business](https://www.suny.edu/meansbusiness/); [SUNY Contracts Search](https://www.suny.edu/business/contractsearch/)). SUNY is therefore not a one-master-many-campuses opportunity; it is 30 separate sales.
- **California State University (CSU)** centralizes through Procurement and Contract Services for system-wide agreements but allows campus-level POs ([CSU CI vendor info](https://www.csuci.edu/financial-services/procurement/vendor-information/index.htm)). The **EdTech JPA** is the California-specific Joint Powers Agency for educational technology master contracts, with 3–4 year RFP cycles ([EdTech JPA](https://edtechjpa.org/services/jpa-contracts); [JPA FAQ](https://edtechjpa.org/about/frequently-asked-questions)).

### Strategic implication

State masters are a year-three or year-four motion for BryantPathfinder. They require a SOC 2 Type II, HECVAT, references, and the ability to respond to a 60-page RFP. The right precursor in 2026–27 is to land **one** anchor institution per region — ideally a small private institution like Bryant in New England, a community college that uses Banner in the Northeast, and one mid-sized state campus that is willing to sign a paid pilot. With three references, a single state-system RFP becomes winnable. Without them, an RFP response gets discarded.

## 5. Title III/V/HEERF and federally-funded pilots

### HEERF
HEERF (CARES, CRRSAA, ARP) ran the largest higher-ed federal cash injection in history but the spending window has effectively closed. Performance periods extended to June 30, 2023 and a small set of institutions received one-time No-Cost Extensions through 2024 ([U.S. Dept of Ed HEERF page](https://www.ed.gov/grants-and-programs/response-programs/covid-19-grants/higher-education-emergency-relief-fund-heerf); [BDO HEERF blog](https://www.bdo.com/insights/blogs/nonprofit-standard/higher-ed-extend-the-heerf-iii-deadline-to-spend-down-funds)). As of April 2026 there is no meaningful HEERF money left to chase. Drop it from the strategy.

### Title III & Title V
Title III Part A (Strengthening Institutions) and Title V (Hispanic-serving institutions and HBCUs/MSIs) are the live federal cash flowing into student-success technology ([U.S. Dept of Ed Title III](https://www.ed.gov/grants-and-programs/grants-higher-education/improvement-of-postsecondary-education/title-iii-part-programs-strengthening-institutions); [NAICU brief](https://www.naicu.edu/policy-advocacy/issue-briefs/institutional-aid-title-iii-and-title-v-institutions/); [Gravyty AI grants guide](https://gravyty.com/blog/ai-education-grants/); [Tarleton State Title III feature](https://www.tarleton.edu/news/title-iii-grant-initiatives-advance-student-success-at-tarleton-state/)). Allowable uses explicitly include:

- Strengthening student-information systems including early-warning systems
- Integrating academic advising and academic support
- Online student services including advising
- Technology endowments for maintenance and upgrades

AI advising tools cleanly qualify under "online student services" and "early-warning systems." The catch: the *institution* applies, not the vendor, and grants run multi-year. The realistic Pathfinder play is to identify five to ten Title III-eligible institutions in New England and the Mid-Atlantic, find ones whose published Title III plans mention advising, and pitch Pathfinder as a sub-allocation of an existing grant. Bryant itself is not Title III-eligible (it does not enroll the required disadvantaged-population proportion), so this is purely a customer-targeting strategy, not a Bryant-pilot strategy. FIPSE Special Projects ([FIPSE-SP November 2025 NOFA](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/11/12/2025-19843/applications-for-new-awards-fund-for-the-improvement-of-postsecondary-education-special-projects)) is a complementary federal source and currently funds AI/advising experimentation at non-MSI institutions.

## 6. The "champion department" wedge

The credible adoption pattern in higher-ed SaaS, repeatedly visible across Stellic, Coursedog, Civitas, and EAB Navigate, is to start with **one** college or department, prove out the workflow, then expand horizontally to a second college and vertically to the registrar/provost ([Stellic case story](https://www.stellic.com/customer-story/quinnipiac-university); [Insider Higher Ed sales blog](https://www.insidehighered.com/blogs/technology-and-learning/ed-tech-sales); [Emerge Insights on university partnerships](https://medium.com/emerge-edtech-insights/how-to-build-a-unicorn-by-partnering-with-universities-to-diversify-their-revenue-streams-part-2-6b3215f26d55)).

For BryantPathfinder this maps cleanly. The pilot wedge should be the **Bryant College of Business** — Owen's home college, AACSB-accredited, finite course list (~80 courses across FIN, ACG, MGT, ENT, etc.), well-defined advising staff, and a population (Owen's classmates) who will literally beta-test in exchange for a sticker. The expansion path inside Bryant is then College of Arts & Sciences and the new School of Health Sciences, which lets Pathfinder prove it generalizes beyond a single curriculum. Outside Bryant, the same wedge applies — pitch Bentley's Business school, Babson's Business school, Providence College's Business school, never the Provost.

The single highest-leverage Bryant ask in 2026 is a **named pilot agreement** with the College of Business that includes (a) opt-in student access for the fall semester, (b) a formal review by the Associate Dean of Advising in December, (c) anonymized pilot metrics that Pathfinder can publish. That single agreement converts the project from "student-built side project" to "Bryant University pilot" on every cold-outreach email, every conference badge, and every NET+ sponsor application.

## 8. Reseller and marketplace partnerships

These programs trade a percentage of revenue or a flat fee for placement in front of the user base of a major incumbent platform. Most have a 6–18 month listing process.

### Ellucian Partner Network — the Banner gateway
The partner network is the only sanctioned path to integrate with Banner, Colleague, or Ethos at scale. Four tracks: Build, Service, Sell, Accelerate ([Ellucian Partners](https://www.ellucian.com/partners); [Partner FAQ PDF](https://www.ellucian.com/assets/en/ellucian-partner-network-faq.pdf); [2024 EPN Partner Guide](https://www.ellucian.com/assets/en/epn-partner-guide.pdf)). The Build track gives technical access to Ethos APIs, sandbox environments, and an integration timeline. Cost is not publicly disclosed but practitioner reports place tier-one Build membership in the $20K–$50K/year annual range plus paid private dev environments. Time-to-listing in the partner catalog runs 9–15 months. **Pathfinder needs this eventually because Banner is the SIS Bryant uses, but the timing should be after multi-tenancy and the Bryant pilot.**

### Anthology Marketplace
The combined Blackboard + Anthology partner ecosystem accepts third-party tools that integrate via LTI 1.3. Listing is generally free for LTI partners but includes a security review. Audience is mostly LMS administrators, not advising staff — moderate fit only.

### Instructure Partner Program (Canvas)
The Edtech Collective Marketplace — a refreshed Canvas-wide partner catalog ([Instructure Partners](https://www.instructure.com/partners); [Edtech Collective program](https://www.instructure.com/press-release/instructure-launches-new-edtech-collective-program-its-community-500-global-partners); [Become an EdCo Partner](https://www.instructure.com/partners/integration-partners/sign-up); [Marketplace roadmap item](https://community.canvaslms.com/t5/RELEASED-Items-Roadmap/Deliver-a-centralized-public-marketplace-for-educators-to-easily/ta-p/610728)). Tiers include "Privacy and Security," "Longevity," and "Support Champion" credentials. Free partner-level entry; LTI integration required. Time-to-listing typically 60–120 days from start of integration work. Bryant uses Canvas, which makes this a cleaner near-term play than Anthology.

### Workday Software Partner Program (Innovation Partners)
([Workday Innovation Partners](https://www.workday.com/en-us/company/partners/innovation-partners.html); [Software Partners](https://www.workday.com/en-us/partners-services/partners/software-partners.html); [Partner Program directory](https://partner-program-directory.partnerfleet.io/partners/workday-innovation-partners-program)). Eligibility requires demonstrated SaaS scalability and security posture plus passing certification. Higher-ed Workday Student footprint is growing but smaller than Banner — relevant only for institutions that have moved off Banner/Colleague. Skip until 2027.

**Realistic 2026 partner moves.** (1) Apply to the Instructure Edtech Collective as a free integration partner once an LTI 1.3 endpoint exists. (2) Apply to Ellucian Partner Network Build track in Q4 2026 once a paid Bryant pilot is in writing. (3) Defer Workday and Anthology.

## 9. The 6-month go-to-market plan for a solo technical founder still in school

Owen's hard constraints: full-time Bryant student through May 2029, on-campus most of the academic year, summer (May–August) is the only blocked-out work-time window, and no co-founder. The plan below treats April 2026 as month zero (this document's authoring date) and runs through October 2026.

### Month 0 — April 2026 (now)
- **Bryant: secure the institutional anchor.** Schedule meetings with (a) the Director or Associate Dean of Academic Advising, (b) the Bryant CIO or Director of Academic Technology, (c) Dean of the College of Business. Pitch the same one-page brief: "Pilot Pathfinder with 50–100 College of Business sophomores in fall 2026, opt-in, no PII handling beyond what students already share." Goal: a signed Memorandum of Understanding with the College of Business by May 1.
- **Incorporate.** Stripe Atlas Delaware C-Corp, $500. Without an LLC/Inc, no master agreement, no NET+ application, no EDSAFE membership.
- **Filing the dummy paperwork.** HECVAT-Lite first draft (use the existing 00-baseline as raw material — most boxes can be answered "No / In progress / Roadmap Q3 2026"). InCommon federation registration started.
- **Apply** to LearnLaunch (rolling) and to the GSV Cup 2027 when window opens.

### Month 1 — May 2026 (finals + start of summer)
- **Run the Bryant pilot prep sprint.** Multi-tenancy spike (one tenant for Bryant), basic auth (magic-link email is acceptable for an opt-in pilot — full SSO can wait), tenant-scoped audit logs, a deletion endpoint, a pilot-results dashboard for the Bryant advisor.
- **Submit a NACADA 2026 concurrent-session proposal** co-authored with a Bryant advisor by NACADA's CFP deadline. Title suggestion: "Building an AI Schedule-Assistant with Your Own Students: Lessons from a Bryant Pilot."
- **Launch a Substack or simple blog.** One post a week describing the build. This is the cheapest possible top-of-funnel; "How I built an AI scheduling tool for my own school's advising office" is a credibility asset.

### Month 2 — June 2026
- **Identify the next 10 schools.** Criteria: small private undergraduate, AACSB-accredited business school, Banner SIS, US Northeast for travel reasons, advising staff active on NACADA listservs. Suggested target list: Bentley, Babson, Providence College, Stonehill, Assumption, Salve Regina, Roger Williams, Suffolk, Saint Anselm, Endicott. ### Month 3 — July 2026
- **Run the second-school discovery.** Goal: convert two of the ten cold emails to a 30-minute call. The ask on each call is *not* "want to buy" — it is "we're shipping Pathfinder for Bryant in the fall, we'd love a video of you walking through your degree audit so we can make sure our solver generalizes." - **Ship Bryant pilot v1** to the College of Business for advisor sign-off.
- **Complete EDSAFE AI Industry Council application.**

### Month 4 — August 2026 (return to Bryant)
- **Soft-launch the pilot.** Email the College of Business sophomore class through the Associate Dean's distribution list. Target: 50 students enrolled within two weeks. Instrument everything. Daily standup with one Bryant advisor.
- **Submit EDUCAUSE 2027 session proposal** when the window opens (typically late summer). Topic: "An AI Course Scheduler That Actually Knows Your Catalog: The Pathfinder Architecture."

### Month 5 — September 2026
- **Pilot data collection.** Targets to publish: % of generated schedules accepted as-is, advisor time saved per student, scheduling-error reduction. NACADA wants three numbers, not seven. Pick the three that look best and publish them.
- **Attend EDUCAUSE Annual** as an attendee (Sep 29 – Oct 2). Twelve coffee chats booked in advance with CIOs on the target list. Goal: two of them ask for a demo.
- **Submit AACRAO 2027 proposal** by their fall CFP.

### Month 6 — October 2026
- **Attend NACADA Annual** (Oct 4–7, Oklahoma City). Either as a presenter (best case) or attendee. Bring 200 one-page Pathfinder briefs, a QR code to a 90-second demo video, and a sign-up form for a "spring 2027 pilot waitlist." Goal: 25 advisors on the waitlist, 5 schools with named pilot interest.
- **Convert pilot data to case study.** A 2-page Bryant case study, designed in the Pathfinder editorial style, becomes the standard collateral for every cold email after this point.
- **Decision point.** If 5+ schools are on the pilot waitlist, file the Internet2 NET+ sponsor request through Bryant. If <5, redirect December–January to product depth (multi-tenancy hardening, SOC 2 Type I close-out) before the next outbound push.

### What this plan deliberately does *not* do
- Hire a salesperson — premature.
- Buy an EDUCAUSE booth — wrong audience for advising and wrong stage.
- Apply for federal grants — Pathfinder is the vendor, not the recipient; institutions apply.
- Pursue the Provost — wrong altitude, wrong door.
- Build SSO/SAML in May — it's not on the demo path for a 50-student opt-in pilot. Magic-link + email allowlist is enough.
- Pitch CSU/SUNY/UNC master agreements — premature without three reference customers.

## Summary recommendation

The single non-obvious distribution insight for BryantPathfinder is that **NACADA, not EDUCAUSE, is the primary 2026 conference**, and **a single named pilot inside the Bryant College of Business is the only thing that matters in the next 90 days.** Everything else — NET+, state masters, Ellucian Partner Network, accelerators, marketplaces — gates on producing one published pilot and one repeatable case study. The Bryant relationship plus the fall-2026 NACADA presentation are the two leverage points that, if executed, unlock the next ten conversations. Without them, no booth, no LinkedIn cold email, and no master-agreement application will work.
