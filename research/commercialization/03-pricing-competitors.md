# 03 — Pricing & Competitors

> Competitive landscape, public-source pricing, and three defensible pricing models for BryantPathfinder. Where pricing is private, comparables are used and labeled as **estimate**.

---

## Competitor profiles

### Stellic (stellic.com)

**What they do.** Stellic is the closest direct competitor to BryantPathfinder. The company replaces or rides alongside Degree Works with a unified degree-audit + degree-planner + course-registration UX. Founded by Carnegie Mellon undergraduates frustrated with legacy planning tools, it lets students drag and drop courses into a multi-semester plan, checks prerequisites in real time, and gives registrars demand-forecasting analytics derived from those plans (https://www.stellic.com, https://www.stellic.com/progress).

**Pricing.** Not public. Stellic uses a "request a quote, sign an MSA + SOW" sales motion with discovery handled by a regional rep; pricing is presumably tiered by FTE plus implementation fees (https://www.stellic.com). The platform is available through E&I Cooperative Services as a negotiated cooperative purchase, which typically signals five- to six-figure annual contracts (https://www.eandi.org/contracts/stellic/). **Estimate:** based on Series-A-stage edtech comparables and the sophistication of the product, ACV likely lands in the **$40K–$150K** range for small/regional schools and **$200K–$500K** for R1s, with one-time implementation fees of $25K–$100K.

**Target segment / customer count.** 45+ institutions disclosed in March 2022 at Series A; current site name-drops Carnegie Mellon, Case Western, University of Wisconsin–Stout, Santa Monica College, Adelphi University, and University of Newcastle (https://www.stellic.com, https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/student-built-degree-progression-platform-raises-11-million-to-empower-students-and-solve-higher-educations-major-challenge-301507815.html). Mix is private R1s, regional publics, and a small number of community colleges.

**Strengths.** Cleanest UX in the category; strong CMU/edtech-mafia investor stack (Reach Capital, ECMC, plus angels including David Blake of Degreed, John Katzman of 2U, Matt Pittinsky of Blackboard) (https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/stellic). Real registrar-side analytics. They've productized the "audit + planner" combo end-to-end.

**Weakness BryantPathfinder can exploit.** Stellic still treats scheduling as a manual drag-and-drop activity. A student picks courses, then has to figure out time slots themselves. BryantPathfinder's deterministic solver returns three conflict-free, ranked, calendar-rendered schedules in two seconds — Stellic does not auto-solve. Owen's product is "the autopilot for the last mile of registration" where Stellic is "a better Degree Works."

### Ellucian Degree Works

**What they do.** The incumbent. Degree Works is the dominant degree-audit engine in U.S. higher ed — installed at roughly two-thirds of four-year institutions, including Bryant. It evaluates a student's transcript against catalog requirements and produces the "what-ifs" and outstanding-requirements view that BryantPathfinder ingests as its input (https://www.ellucian.com/solutions/ellucian-degree-works, https://www.ellucian.com/assets/en/solution-sheet/ellucian-degree-works.pdf).

**Pricing.** Sold as a module on top of Banner SIS. Not separately priced publicly. The UK Crown Commercial G-Cloud framework lists Banner SaaS at **£335,000/year for a 5,000 FTE** institution (~$420K USD), with Degree Works typically a **15–25% add-on module** (https://assets.applytosupply.digitalmarketplace.service.gov.uk/g-cloud-14/documents/706599/182234470153077-pricing-document-2024-05-03-1221.pdf). Capterra/ITQlick comparables put small-college Banner license costs at $20K–$50K/year for sub-100-user institutions and $50K–$200K for implementation (https://www.itqlick.com/banner-by-ellucian/pricing). **Estimate:** standalone Degree Works ACV is **$25K–$80K for small colleges, $80K–$250K for regionals, $250K–$600K for R1s**.

**Target segment.** Universal. ~1,500+ Degree Works installations globally per Ellucian's own materials.

**Strengths.** Embedded in registrar workflow. Already has the authoritative graduation-requirement rule set. Single-vendor relationship with Banner SIS means data integration is "free." Procurement teams trust Ellucian.

**Weakness BryantPathfinder can exploit.** Degree Works is famously ugly and famously confusing to students — every campus runs help-desk traffic asking "what does this audit mean?" Degree Works tells students *what* is missing; it does not tell them *which sections to take this fall to fix it*. BryantPathfinder positions as a **ride-along, not replacement** — read Degree Works output, do the work Degree Works refuses to do.

### EAB Navigate360 (eab.com/navigate)

**What they do.** Navigate360 is the dominant student-success and advising CRM in U.S. higher ed: case management for advisors, early-alert flags, appointment scheduling, mass-text "nudges" to at-risk students, and a student-facing mobile app for to-do lists and class schedules (https://eab.com/solutions/navigate360/). It does *not* generate schedules — it surfaces holds and registration reminders.

**Pricing.** Not published. Enterprise license model — one fee covers all staff and students at the institution, no per-seat surcharges (https://www.g2.com/products/eab-navigate360/reviews). Public procurement disclosure: **Austin Peay State University (~10,500 FTE) pays $278,000/year** for Navigate, projecting an $0.8M ROI by Fall 2023 and $2.5M by Fall 2027 (https://govstech.apsu.edu/TDClient/2071/Portal/Projects/Details/?TID=428775). The University of Wisconsin System renewed its EAB contract for five years in 2023 covering multiple campuses (https://www.wisconsin.edu/ss-eab-project/). **Estimate:** Navigate360 ACV is roughly **$25–$30 per FTE/year**, scaling discount with size: ~$120K–$180K for a 5K FTE community college, ~$280K–$350K for a 10–12K regional, ~$700K–$1.0M for a 30–40K R1.

**Target segment / customer count.** 850+ institutions across two- and four-year segments; 450+ community/technical colleges on the Navigate360 Advantage variant (https://eab.com/solutions/navigate360-advantage-for-community-colleges/).

**Strengths.** Procurement gravity — most CFOs already have a Navigate line item. Massive advisor adoption. Bundled with EAB's research/best-practices brand.

**Weakness BryantPathfinder can exploit.** EAB Navigate is built for the **advisor**, not the student. The student app is a thin to-do list with no scheduling intelligence. BryantPathfinder is built for the student first, and gives advisors the upside (fewer "help me pick classes" appointments) as a side effect. Different DAU pattern, different ROI story.

### Civitas Learning

**What they do.** Predictive analytics for student persistence, completion, and intervention effectiveness. Models trained on each institution's own historical data ("How likely is this student to retain? Which initiatives actually work?") (https://www.civitaslearning.com/, https://www.civitaslearning.com/analytics/).

**Pricing.** Not public. Model is upfront implementation fee (data integration, model training) plus annual license (https://www.softwaresuggest.com/civitas-learning). **Estimate:** $80K–$300K ACV depending on institution size and data complexity, with $50K–$150K implementation.

**Target segment / customer count.** 400+ colleges/universities serving 8M+ students per their own claim (https://www.civitaslearning.com/).

**Strengths.** Genuinely sophisticated ML stack. "Initiative analysis" — measuring what programs actually move retention — is a hard sell others can't match.

**Weakness BryantPathfinder can exploit.** Civitas tells institutions *which students are at risk*; it does not give those students a tool. It is fundamentally backoffice. BryantPathfinder is the front-of-house product the at-risk student actually uses, and could plausibly *be* a Civitas intervention rather than compete with it.

### Watermark (watermarkinsights.com)

**What they do.** Academic operations and assessment suite — Planning & Self-Study (accreditation), Course Evaluation & Surveys, Faculty Success (faculty activity reporting), Outcomes Assessment, Curriculum Strategy, Student Learning & Licensure (https://www.watermarkinsights.com/, https://www.watermarkinsights.com/solutions/).

**Pricing.** Not public. Capterra range "$10–$100" (likely per-user/month for some component, ambiguous) (https://www.capterra.com/p/211854/Watermark/). **Estimate:** $30K–$200K ACV across the modular suite, depending on how many of the seven products an institution buys.

**Target segment / customer count.** 1,700+ institutions globally per the company (https://www.watermarkinsights.com/).

**Strengths.** Owns accreditation workflows — sticky compliance use case. Multi-product cross-sell.

**Weakness BryantPathfinder can exploit.** Watermark is institutional plumbing that students never see. They have zero student-facing UX equity and no obvious path to it. Adjacent, not competitive.

---

### Coursedog

**What they do.** Integrated academic operations platform — course/section scheduling, room scheduling, curriculum management, course-demand projection, online catalog. Bi-directional integration with the SIS (https://www.coursedog.com/, https://www.coursedog.com/products/academic-course-scheduling-software).

**Pricing.** Not public. Enterprise custom pricing (https://www.capterra.com/p/199011/Coursedog/). **Estimate:** $60K–$300K ACV depending on which modules, comparable to Ad Astra.

**Target segment / customer count.** ~170 institutional customers including CUNY, Columbia, BYU, Dallas Baptist, Brookdale Community College (https://www.coursedog.com/articles/coursedog-raises-17m-to-break-down-barriers-in-higher-education). Raised **$112M total**, including a $90M JMI Equity strategic growth round (https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/coursedog, https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/267013-36).

**Strengths.** Well-funded. Solves the *registrar's* scheduling problem (where to put which section in which room) — a real pain.

**Weakness BryantPathfinder can exploit.** Coursedog optimizes the institution's schedule. It does *not* help an individual student build their personal schedule from the catalog. Different actor, different problem. The two could theoretically integrate (Coursedog provides the section catalog; BryantPathfinder solves the student's problem on top of it).

### Pathify

**What they do.** Campus Experience Platform (CXP) — replaces fragmented portals, intranets, and mobile apps with one unified hub aggregating SIS, LMS, CRM, and ERP data (https://pathify.com/).

**Pricing.** Not public; available through E&I Cooperative and Internet2 NET+ for negotiated pricing (https://www.eandi.org/contracts/pathify/, https://internet2.edu/services/pathify/). **Estimate:** $40K–$200K ACV by FTE.

**Target segment / customer count.** 250+ institutions including Johnson & Wales, Utah State, Middlebury College, Ventura County Community College (https://pathify.com/).

**Strengths.** Wide installation base. Already the homepage students log into.

**Weakness BryantPathfinder can exploit.** Pathify is a portal — a tile launcher for other apps. It doesn't *do* anything itself. BryantPathfinder is a product, not a tile, and could plausibly launch *inside* Pathify rather than against it.

---

### Othot (Liaison)

**What they do.** Predictive analytics for enrollment yield, financial-aid optimization, retention, and persistence. ML models trained per-institution (https://www.othot.com/, https://www.liaisonedu.com/higher-education-ai/othot/).

**Pricing.** Not public. 30–45 day onboarding cycle (https://www.liaisonedu.com/higher-education-ai/othot/). **Estimate:** $50K–$250K ACV; sold most aggressively to admissions/enrollment-management offices.

**Target segment.** Enrollment offices at private regionals and graduate-professional schools. Recently launched "Othot for AACOMAS" for osteopathic medical schools (https://www.liaisonedu.com/resources/news/liaison-and-aacom-partner-to-launch-othot-for-aacomas-bringing-predictive-analytics-and-holistic-review-to-osteopathic-medical-schools/).

**Strengths.** Owned by Liaison (CAS, the application-portal incumbent), so deeply embedded in admissions data flow.

**Weakness BryantPathfinder can exploit.** Othot stops at the deposit. Once a student enrolls, Othot has no product for them. The handoff from Othot's "yield" model to a tool that helps the deposited student actually plan their first semester is a gap BryantPathfinder fills.

### Element451

**What they do.** AI-first student-engagement CRM with Bolt Agents — agentic AI workforce that reads applications, sends nudges, and flags risk. Covers admissions, marketing, engagement, and student-success modules (https://element451.com/, https://element451.com/ai-student-engagement-crm).

**Pricing.** **Public starting price: $20,000–$40,000+ annually for institutions under 10,000 students** (https://saascrmreview.com/element451-review/, https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/intelligent-crm-leader-element451-unveils-new-packages-and-pricing-for-ai-powered-enrollment-engagement-and-student-success-301974579.html). Modular pricing: Element Core (foundation) + Marketing / Engagement / Admissions / Success modules.

**Target segment / customer count.** 300+ institutions, 60M+ student journeys (https://element451.com/about-us). Strongest in admissions/recruitment.

**Strengths.** Most aggressive "AI agent" branding in the category. Modular pricing reads as accessible vs. EAB.

**Weakness BryantPathfinder can exploit.** Element451's "AI advising" is conversational — a chatbot that nudges a student to fill out a form. BryantPathfinder produces a *deterministic, conflict-free, calendar-rendered schedule with CRNs* — a tangible artifact, not a chat. Anti-chatbot UX is a genuine wedge in 2026.

---

### Mainstay (formerly AdmitHub)

**What they do.** Behaviorally intelligent SMS chatbot for student engagement. Now positions as "human-centered, AI-enhanced" student-success coaching (https://mainstay.com/).

**Pricing.** Not public. Available through NJEdge consortium (https://edgemarket.njedge.net/home/mainstay-engagement-platform). **Estimate:** $25K–$120K ACV typically structured per-student or per-message-volume.

**Target segment / customer count.** Hundreds of institutions and college-access networks; 5M+ students supported per the company (https://mainstay.com/, https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/admithub).

**Strengths.** Owns "summer melt" use case — texting deposited students before they ghost. Strong evidence-based research grounding (Lindsay Page et al. studies).

**Weakness BryantPathfinder can exploit.** Same as Element451 — chatbots are perceived as friction, not solution. Mainstay can answer "what do I need to register?" but cannot register the student. BryantPathfinder produces the artifact a chatbot can only describe.

---

### Ad Astra (aais.com)

**What they do.** Smart Scheduling for higher ed — section/room/event scheduling, registration monitoring, long-term academic planning (https://www.aais.com/, https://www.aais.com/solutions). Institution-side, not student-facing.

**Pricing.** Not public. Available via AWS Marketplace as a Private Offer (https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/pp/prodview-iakwaglrhci7a). **Estimate:** $50K–$300K ACV; Capterra and FitGap reviewers describe it as "significant total cost of ownership."

**Target segment / customer count.** 550+ institutions, especially mid/large publics (https://www.aais.com/).

**Strengths.** Best-in-class room/space optimization. Provost-office credibility.

**Weakness BryantPathfinder can exploit.** Same shape as Coursedog — Ad Astra optimizes the institution's schedule, not the student's. Adjacent, not competitive. A potential channel partner: Ad Astra's section-demand data could feed BryantPathfinder's ranking logic.

## Positioning quadrant

X-axis: **Institutional analytics / back-office** ← → **Student-facing planner / front-of-house**
Y-axis: **SMB / cheap (sub-$100K ACV)** ↑↓ **Enterprise (>$200K ACV)**

| | Institutional / Back-office | Student-facing / Front-of-house |
|---|---|---|
| **Enterprise (>$200K ACV)** | Ellucian Degree Works, EAB Navigate360, Civitas Learning, Coursedog, Ad Astra, Othot, Watermark | Stellic (R1 deals), Pathify (large deployments) |
| **SMB / sub-$100K ACV** | Mainstay (consortium pricing), Watermark (single-module), Othot (small grad programs) | **BryantPathfinder (target)**, Element451 ($20–40K starting), Stellic (small-college deals), Pathify (small) |

BryantPathfinder's open quadrant is **bottom-right: cheap, student-facing, focused**. Stellic is the only direct neighbor; Element451 and Pathify are CRMs/portals, not planners. The middle of the matrix — enterprise back-office — is saturated and gated by procurement. The bottom-right is contested mostly by free in-house tools and Stellic's small-school deals.

## Three pricing models for BryantPathfinder

Anthropic API cost assumption from the baseline: **5–10¢ per active session**. Assume 8 sessions/student/year (registration + add-drop windows), so **$0.40–$0.80 in COGS per student/year**. Hosting/infra adds another ~$0.20/student/year at scale. Implementation/CSM labor adds ~$10–$20K per institution amortized over multi-year contract.

### Model A — Per-FTE annual

| Tier | Price | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Per-undergrad-FTE/year | **$8** | Below EAB's ~$25–$30/FTE; positions as a "$0.67/student/month" line item — easier than CFO scrutiny on a $200K platform |

| Institution | FTE | ACV | Anthropic COGS | Gross margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Community college | 5,000 | $40,000 | $2,000–$4,000 | ~92% |
| Regional private | 12,000 | $96,000 | $4,800–$9,600 | ~92% |
| R1 public | 35,000 | $280,000 | $14,000–$28,000 | ~92% |

**Objection it answers.** "How does this compare to our existing student-success spend?" A 12K-FTE regional already pays EAB ~$300K/year; adding BryantPathfinder at $96K is a 30% bolt-on, not a new line of business. CFO-defensible.

### Model B — Flat institutional tier (Bronze / Silver / Gold)

| Tier | Eligibility | Price |
|---|---|---|
| Bronze | <3,000 FTE | **$25,000/year** |
| Silver | 3,000–15,000 FTE | **$75,000/year** |
| Gold | 15,000+ FTE | **$200,000/year + $5/FTE over 30K** |

| Institution | FTE | Tier | ACV | COGS | Gross margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Community college | 5,000 | Silver | $75,000 | $2,000–$4,000 | ~95% |
| Regional private | 12,000 | Silver | $75,000 | $4,800–$9,600 | ~88% |
| R1 public | 35,000 | Gold | $225,000 | $14,000–$28,000 | ~89% |

**Objection it answers.** "I can't get a per-FTE deal through procurement; we don't budget that way." Flat tiers map cleanly to the "site license" mental model procurement teams already use for EAB and Pathify. Easier to sign.

### Model C — Freemium-to-institutional

- **Free** for individual students. Anyone with a Bryant (or partner-institution) email can use the planner.
- **Institutional upgrade** unlocks: (a) advisor seats with caseload views and "what schedules are my advisees considering" dashboards; (b) SIS write-back / one-click registration; (c) FERPA-compliant data residency and SSO; (d) institutional analytics — section-demand projections, bottleneck reports, "students stuck on FIN 310" alerts.

| Tier | Price |
|---|---|
| Free student | $0 |
| Institutional upgrade | **$15K base + $4/student/year** |

| Institution | FTE | ACV | COGS | Gross margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Community college | 5,000 | $35,000 | $2,000–$4,000 + ~$1,500 free-tier overhead | ~84% |
| Regional private | 12,000 | $63,000 | $4,800–$9,600 + ~$3,500 free-tier overhead | ~80% |
| R1 public | 35,000 | $155,000 | $14,000–$28,000 + ~$10K free-tier overhead | ~75% |

**Objection it answers.** "I need to see student adoption before I'll champion procurement." Freemium shifts the proof burden — the school sees students *already* using BryantPathfinder before the contract conversation, the way Notion/Linear/Figma penetrate enterprises. It's the only one of the three that creates inbound demand from the student body. Margin is lower (free tier consumes Anthropic credits with no offset until institutional conversion) but the CAC story is meaningfully better.

**Recommended primary model:** **Model B (flat tiers)** for first 5 institutions because procurement-friendliness matters most when the founder has no enterprise sales team. **Model C (freemium)** as the long-term motion once the product has 1+ paid institution as a reference and Owen has either a co-founder or a partial sales hire. **Model A (per-FTE)** is the eventual "growth-stage" pricing.

## Positioning vs. Degree Works — ride-along, not replacement

Degree Works owns one job and owns it well: it is the source-of-truth audit engine. It evaluates a student's transcript against institutional graduation requirements, surfaces what is unmet, and lives inside the registrar's procurement and governance. **BryantPathfinder does not try to replace Degree Works.** It reads Degree Works output (via screenshot today, via official Ellucian Ethos or Banner export tomorrow), trusts the audit as authoritative, and does the work Degree Works was never built to do — turn "you still need a 4000-level finance elective" into "take FIN 442 with Professor X on TR 9:30–10:50 and you'll be home Fridays." This is the **last-mile autopilot** for a system that today only tells the student where they need to go, never how to get there. Selling against Degree Works would mean a multi-year RFP, an Ellucian competitive response, and an integration battle Owen cannot win solo. Selling *with* Degree Works means BryantPathfinder is a **complement** in procurement language — a separate budget line, a separate champion (the dean of students or VP of student success, not the registrar), and a wedge that doesn't threaten the incumbent.

## Three wedges — adjacent micro-problems BryantPathfinder solves better

1. **The conflict-free schedule wedge.** No competitor in the table generates ranked, time-conflict-free, calendar-rendered schedules from a degree audit. Stellic plans courses; BryantPathfinder plans *minutes*. Defendable feature: the deterministic Python solver (ADR-0003) — it physically cannot return a conflicted schedule, where any LLM-only competitor (Element451, Mainstay) eventually will.

2. **The professor-fit wedge.** No competitor surfaces RateMyProfessors data + historical grade distributions inline at scheduling time. Bryant students Google professors before registering; BryantPathfinder collapses that two-tab workflow into one ranked card. Defendable feature: the Professor Match Agent, which is already populated for 97% of Bryant instructors and is a one-week scrape per new institution.

3. **The negotiation wedge.** When a student over-constrains their preferences ("no Friday classes, no 8 AMs, no MWF, must be 15 credits"), every other tool either returns zero results or silently relaxes a constraint without telling the student. BryantPathfinder's Negotiator Agent finds the *minimum* relaxation needed and explains it in a sentence ("If you allow one Friday class, four schedules open up"). Defendable feature: this is a non-trivial constraint-satisfaction explanation problem, and it's already built. Stellic, EAB, and Element451 cannot do it, and a chatbot competitor would have to invoke a Python solver to do it correctly, which puts them on BryantPathfinder's home turf.
